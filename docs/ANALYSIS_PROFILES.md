# Analysis Profiles

Analysis profiles provide **small sets of specialist fields** without turning every catalogue record into a specialist database.

They are intentionally secondary to the core record.

## How activation works

A profile can be:

- automatically suggested from **Collection Area**;
- automatically suggested from one or more **Classifications**;
- automatically suggested from **Object Type**;
- manual only.

Staff may also add an available profile manually when an object needs it.

## Starter profiles

### Art & Works on Paper
Useful fields include work type, support/substrate, style/movement, school/workshop, edition/state, orientation, and signature/artist mark.

### Sculpture
Sculpture type, process, base/mount, surface/finish, component count, and cast/edition information.

### Historic Furniture
Furniture type, primary wood/species, construction, joinery, upholstery, finish, hardware/fittings, and set relationship.

### Textiles & Costume
Textile type, fibre detail, construction, weave/structure, decoration, pattern/motif, and garment/component.

### Historic Tools & Implements
Functional category, trade/occupation, power source, working end, handle/grip, mechanism, maker/model, and patent/serial number.

### Agriculture & Rural Life
Implement type, agricultural activity, power/draft, crop/livestock association, working part, regional/local name, maker/model, and patent/serial number.

### Indigenous Cultural Context
Community-preferred name, attribution certainty, Indigenous-language object name, language, community use/meaning, cultural sensitivity, access/display guidance, consultation/source, and return/repatriation status.

This profile appears when staff intentionally apply `Indigenous Material Culture`. It must **not** be used to infer identity from appearance. Local/community terminology and consultation take precedence over generic software defaults.

### Historic Photography
Photographic process, format, support/mount, positive/negative/transparency, studio/maker, housing, orientation, and annotations/marks.

### Jewellery & Personal Adornment
Adornment type, body/wear location, fastening, setting/mounting, stones/inlays, and hallmarks.

### Arms & Armour
Category/form, component, mechanism/action, calibre/gauge, maker/model, serial/proof/inspection marks, and associated equipment.

### Archaeology & Excavated Material
Context type, site/feature/context number, diagnostic type, ware/industry, completeness, surface treatment, residue/use-wear, and field/catalogue number.

### Ceramics & Glass
Body/material detail, forming method, surface treatment, glaze/finish, decoration, firing/manufacture notes, and maker/factory mark.

### Natural History
A compact specimen profile: specimen type, scientific/common name, identification qualifier, taxonomy, specimen count, collector/event, verbatim locality, geological context, and label transcription.

Natural History is **one analytical profile among the others**. It is not a separate section of the CMS.

## Design principle

Do not duplicate information unnecessarily. For example, a Furniture profile should not repeat every Material or Technique already recorded in the core record. Use the profile only for analytical detail that improves understanding or retrieval.
